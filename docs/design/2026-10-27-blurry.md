# DeepLabeling — Blurry boxes

**Status:** approved 2026-10-27 (the user takes the recommended calls)
**Scope:** a third queue source beside Images and Find Bad: every label box scored for
sharpness, ranked against boxes of its own class and size, the blurriest listed for review in
the crop grid and the full frame. It builds on [Find Bad](2026-10-06-find-bad.md): its items
are findings of a new kind, so crop tiles, the spotlight, dismissals and multi-tile actions
are Find Bad's, taught the new kind where they name kinds ([what changes](#what-changes-in-find-bads-code)).
Everything else stays as it is.

## Why

Video-sourced datasets hold objects too blurred to label with confidence: motion blur,
defocus, a head three pixels across smeared by compression. The user wants to see them,
ranked, and decide what to do with each. A spike on `pvn-person/data_040226.yaml`
(2026-10-26/27, 5,000 boxes of 1,587 images) settled the measure; its numbers are below.

**Blurry is not wrong.** A camera that produces blurry heads at night needs a model that
detects blurry heads, and a deleted label leaves its object in the image as background — the
model is then taught that a blurry head is nothing. The app ranks and shows; it never
removes anything on its own, and the queue editor says what deleting does.

## Decisions

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| How blurry boxes are served | **A third queue source, `Blurry`**, whose items are findings of kind `blurry`: Find Bad's crop tiles, spotlight, dismissals, badges and multi-tile actions |
| What a box is compared with | **Boxes of its class and size octave, in the queue's scope**: the scope's splits and conditions, as Find Bad's |
| The measure | **The average of two robust z-scores**: a tiled Laplacian ratio and Crété-Roffet (below) |
| What is listed | **The blurriest share of each class-and-size group**, default 5% |
| Removing | **No new action.** Delete, the class keys, dismiss and mark reviewed, as in Find Bad; the editor warns that a deleted label becomes background |
| Where scores live | **`sharpness.sqlite` in the dataset's state folder**, per image and stamp, written on the GUI thread |
| Dependencies | **None new**: OpenCV and numpy, already the app's. Crété-Roffet is ported from scikit-image, which the app does not depend on |

## The measure

Computed on the box's own pixels, in grey, at the image's resolution as shown (EXIF
orientation applied, as `cv2.imread` does). The box's pixel rectangle is its corners times
the frame's size, rounded and clipped to the frame — the rule
`src/deeplabeling/core/crops.py::crop_rect` uses for the label, before its padding. Its
**short side** is the smaller of that rectangle's width and height.

- **Tiled Laplacian ratio.** Smooth the crop with a Gaussian of σ 0.7 (noise is not detail),
  take its Laplacian. Over windows of `t = max(8, short side // 3)` px at steps of `t // 2`,
  take each window's Laplacian variance over its grey variance; skip windows whose grey
  standard deviation is under 5 (flat: wall, floor, skin); keep the 80th percentile, as
  `log10`. With no window left, the whole crop's ratio, its grey variance plus 1 as the
  divisor. An object is as sharp as its detailed parts, not its plain background.
- **A flat crop** — grey standard deviation under 2 over the whole box: a black frame, a
  solid patch — has nothing to measure. It is not scored for the queue and is counted
  `flat`; ranking it blurriest would fill the queue with dark frames.
- **Crété-Roffet** (`skimage.measure.blur_effect`, h_size 11, max over the two axes),
  negated so higher is sharper. Ported to OpenCV: the port must equal scikit-image to 1e-6
  on real crops, checked by a test that skips when scikit-image is absent.
- **Robust z within the group:** for each of the two, `(value − median) / (1.4826 · MAD)`
  over the group, the divisor at least 1e-6 (a group of equal values: every z is 0); the
  score is their average. Lower is blurrier.
- **Groups:** a box's class, and the octave of its short side in px: 8–15, 16–31, 32–63,
  64–127, 128+. A box under the minimum side is not scored for the queue; a group under 20
  boxes is not ranked. Both are counted.
- **Peers** are every scored box of the group in the scope — dismissed ones included, so a
  dismissal never moves another box's rank.
- **Listed:** each group's boxes ordered by score, ties by dataset order and line; the first
  `share` of them, rounded up; then the dismissed among those are left out. Dismissing does
  not pull the next box in: to see further down a group, raise the share. Its **rank** is
  its place in its group as a fraction: 0.004 is blurrier than 99.6% of its peers.
- **The scope selects images, as Find Bad's does**: every box of a selected image is ranked
  against its class and size. A *Classes: has head* row selects images with a head; their
  other boxes are ranked too, each against its own class. A scope that changes as you work
  (*Reviewed: not reviewed*) changes the peers at the next rebuild.

Why these two, from the spike (blur added to real crops; AUC = how often an untouched crop
of one object outranks a blurred crop of another, same class and size; shift = how far the
score moves, in z, when nothing was blurred):

| Measure | AUC σ 1 | AUC σ 2 | AUC motion 9 px | Shift: JPEG q25 | noise σ 8 | contrast ½ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Laplacian / variance, whole crop | 0.970 | 0.998 | 0.947 | −0.24 | +0.87 | 0.00 |
| Crété-Roffet | 0.839 | 0.985 | 0.974 | +0.01 | +0.36 | +0.01 |
| tiled ratio + Crété-Roffet (chosen) | 0.925 | 0.997 | 0.976 | −0.04 | +0.39 | −0.01 |

The plain Laplacian variance ranked dark night crops blurriest and burned-in timestamps
sharpest; contrast normalisation fixed the first, the tiles the second and large boxes of
plain background. Noise still reads partly as detail, and over-sharpened CCTV crops rank
sharpest: only the blurry end is used. A smooth gradient (uneven light) behind sparse detail
keeps its tiles — they have grey variance — and pulls the score down; on real crops the object
fills most of its box, which is what the spike measured.

### Settings

Saved with the queue, edited in the queue editor:

| Setting | Default | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| share | 5% | above 0, at most 100 | how much of each group is listed |
| minimum side | 16 px | 8 to 4096 | smaller boxes are too small to judge |

## The score cache

- **`sharpness.sqlite`** in the dataset's state folder: one row per image — its
  dataset-relative path, its stamp (size, mtime_ns, as the prediction store's) and JSON
  `[[x0, y0, x1, y1, lap, cr], …]` for every box of 8 px or more, by its pixel rectangle.
  `PRAGMA user_version` 1; another version, or a corrupt file, is set aside and started
  again, as the prediction store is; a read-only file is read as it is.
- **A box's scores are found by its pixel rectangle**: a class change needs no rescore; a
  moved or resized box, a new one, or a changed image does, and then the whole image is
  scored again.
- The store's connection handling, setting aside and closing are the prediction store's,
  shared, not copied.
- **Writes happen on the GUI thread** ([workers.py](../../src/deeplabeling/ui/workers.py)'s
  rule: a job never writes a file): the build emits each batch of new scores, and the
  session writes it in one transaction — **even when the batch comes from a build that has
  since been cancelled or replaced**, while the same dataset is open: that is how a
  cancelled build's work is kept. (Item batches of an old queue are still dropped, as
  `src/deeplabeling/ui/session.py::Session._grew` drops them.) A write that fails says so once
  in a notice; the build goes on with the scores it has.
- Size: about 60 bytes a box — 360k boxes take about 20 MB.

## The queue

- **The `Blurry` source** holds the scope (an `ImageFilter`) and the settings; saved as
  `{"source": "blurry", "scope": …, "settings": …}`. It needs no model.
- **The build runs on the build worker.** For each image the scope selects: its labels; its
  cached scores when the stamp matches and every box of 8 px or more is there; otherwise the
  image is decoded and scored. Then groups, z-scores and ranks; items are emitted ordered by
  rank, then score, then dataset order and line.
- **While it builds**, the queue chip says `scoring 1,200 / 19,499`; at the end it counts
  what was listed and what could not be settled: `· 412 blurry · 3,018 too small · 2 cannot
  decode`. Groups too small to rank are counted as `too few peers`, and flat crops as
  `flat`, by box.
- **An item** is a `Finding` of kind `blurry` — line, text, label class and box, no model box
  — with its rank, its z-score and its group's name (`head 16–31 px`). Its key is
  `rel#line#blurry`; its focus is its label line; its crop is the label's, padded as Find
  Bad's.
- **Kept current:** after an edit, an item holds while its line's text is still in the file;
  otherwise it shows *fixed*. A dismissal shows *dismissed*. Neither leaves the queue until
  it is rebuilt. The ranking is not recomputed after an edit.
- **Cancelling** (a new queue, closing the dataset) stops between images; the scores
  written so far are kept, so the next build resumes.

## Window

- **A row** reads `camA/0001.jpg — blurry: head, 0.4% of 16–31 px`; the inspector's
  *Finding* line `blurry · 0.4% of head 16–31 px · z −3.4`; a crop tile's caption
  `head 0.4%`.
- **The spotlight** dims outside the crop, as Find Bad's; there is no model box to draw.
- **Keys:** `D` dismisses, `Delete` and the class keys edit, `Space`/`K` mark reviewed, as in
  Find Bad. `M` and `B` say `not for blurry boxes` and change nothing.
- **The grid** shows crop tiles, as for Find Bad; multi-tile actions are one edit.
- **The queue editor:** *Source: Images / Find Bad / Blurry.* Blurry adds a group titled
  "Blurry — the blurriest of each class and size": *share* (%) and *minimum side* (px),
  refused by name as the other rows are, and a line under them: *A deleted label leaves its
  object as background.*
- The queue chip's menu, statistics and reassign work on a Blurry queue's images as on Find
  Bad's ([the queue's images](2026-10-07-bulk-and-stats.md#the-queues-images)).

## What changes in Find Bad's code

Find Bad's views name its kinds; each learns `blurry`:

- `src/deeplabeling/ui/findview.py::finding_text`, `finding_caption` and `finding_detail`
  (they format a model confidence a blurry box has not got);
- crop-tile mode, `src/deeplabeling/ui/queue_panel.py::QueuePanel._on_crops`, and the chip's
  counts, `src/deeplabeling/ui/session.py::Session.progress_text`, which test for the
  `Findings` source;
- `M` and `B`, in the full frame (`src/deeplabeling/ui/editing.py`) and the grid
  (`src/deeplabeling/ui/grid_actions.py`): `not for blurry boxes`;
- the session's handling of the build's emitted batches, for score batches (above).

## Errors

| Situation | What happens | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| An image cannot be decoded | its boxes are left out; counted `cannot decode` | queue chip |
| A box's crop is flat | left out; counted `flat` | queue chip |
| A label file cannot be read | left out; counted `labels unreadable`, as elsewhere | queue chip |
| `sharpness.sqlite` is corrupt or of another version | set aside, started again | notice |
| A score write fails (disk full) | said once; the build goes on | notice |
| `M` or `B` on a blurry box | nothing changes | status bar |

## Performance targets

Measured on `pvn-person/data_040226.yaml` (19,499 images, about 28 boxes an image), on this
machine. A missed target is recorded with its measured value, not quietly dropped.

| Action | Target | Measured 2026-10-27, in the window: 21,819 listed of 416k boxes, 116,514 too small |
|---|---|---|
| a cold build over the whole dataset | ≤ 6 min | 263 s (the spike's one-thread estimate: 3.7 min) |
| a warm build (every image cached) | ≤ 10 s | 4.7 s |
| the window while it builds | the event loop never waits more than 100 ms | **missed: 191 ms cold, 170 ms warm** |
| a page of crop tiles | ≤ 1 s | 66 ms, 24 tiles |

The missed target, as it stands:

- **While it scores, the window keeps its turns.** The first measurement waited 233 ms cold
  and 224 ms warm: full garbage collections over a build's hundreds of thousands of
  candidates (195–280 ms each, freeing nothing), a 500-image score write on the GUI thread
  (202 ms), and once a 1.8 s stall, most likely a WAL checkpoint's sync. Candidates are now
  plain tuples the collector stops tracking
  (`src/deeplabeling/core/blurry.py::candidate`), groups are sorted in numpy, scores go out
  50 images at a time, and the cache never syncs (`SharpnessStore.SYNCHRONOUS`).
- **What is left is once, at the end of a build:** creating the 21,819 items and handing
  them to the window (gaps of 115–135 ms), and a full collection there when one falls due
  (179–193 ms) — the app-wide stall also seen while a large dataset opens.

A **read-only measurement script**, `tools/measure_blurry.py`, reproduces them with app state
in a temporary folder, as `tools/measure_find_bad.py` does.

## Testing

- **Core, on the generated edge-case dataset and synthetic crops:** the tiled ratio and
  Crété-Roffet lower on a Gaussian-blurred copy than on the original; flat tiles skipped; the
  Crété-Roffet port equal to scikit-image (skipped without it); groups by class and octave;
  z by median and MAD, and a group of equal values; the share rounded up per group, taken
  before dismissed boxes are left out; too small, flat and too few peers counted;
  a box found in the cache by its pixel rectangle, a class change needing no rescore, a
  moved box or a changed image rescoring; a corrupt or other-version cache set aside; the
  source's JSON round trip and refusal of bad settings; a cancelled build keeping its
  scores; dismissals by `(image, line text, "blurry")`.
- **UI:** the Source switch and the Blurry group's refusals; crop tiles of a Blurry queue
  read back from their pixels; the row, inspector and caption texts; `D`, `Delete` and a
  multi-tile delete as one edit, undone whole; `M`/`B` refused; *fixed* after an edit; the
  chip's progress and counts; scores written on the GUI thread and reused on a rebuild, and
  a cancelled build's scores kept.
- **The measurement script**, for the targets.

## Out of scope

- Leaving an image out of training, or masking a region: no such mechanism exists in the
  app, and it is the user's decision what blurry objects should become.
- Scoring images as a whole, near-duplicates, exposure: other measures.
- Recomputing ranks as edits happen: a rebuild does.
- A scoring pass separate from the queue build.
